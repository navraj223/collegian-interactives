THE WAY WE HALLOWEEN IS CHANGING

A familiar night.
A different doorstep.

From front porches to decorated trunks, the route to a bag of candy is changing.

01 / THE PORCH

It used to start
with a doorbell.

Remember going door-to-door for Halloween candy? Well, that looks a little different now.

There’s a rising method called trunk-or-treating, which involves none other than a car. At trunk-or-treat events, children go from car to car collecting candy from decorated trunks.

Using the trunks of a few Halloween-decorated cars, community organizations, local institutions and parent clubs have made the spooky holiday a bit different than what we remember.

But what about the doorbell? What about going up to an actual house, pressing that little button and hearing the sound of the bell flow through the house from the outside while you wait for the door to open?

THE DOORBELL DAYS

Some traditions
stay with us.

“I personally prefer the traditional trick-or-treating because of the nostalgia of it,” said Eva Mihalcin, Fresno State student.

Mihalcin said she has only ever been to one or two trunk-or-treat events.

“It’s a mix of good and bad,” she said. “Good because it’s probably a safer option than letting kids go out to trick-or-treat nowadays, but I guess more of bad because the nostalgia of going out and trick-or-treating is a pretty big thing.”

For many people born in the early 2000s or earlier, going door-to-door with friends or family brings back childhood Halloween memories.

But for the younger kids today, trunk-or-treating will inevitably be their reminiscent version of what we experienced.

“It’s more of an interactive experience for the kids,” said Rayder Silva, another student at Fresno State.

A DIFFERENT KIND OF DOOR

New routes.
New memories.

Rather than going long distances in various neighborhoods, Silva thinks trunk-or-treating is a better approach to them enjoying the holiday.

Art major Dora Youngblood said having candy set out by the doors, watching kids come by in their costumes and interacting with them are why she prefers door-to-door trick-or-treating. Even her grandkids enjoy participating in it.

03 / THE NEIGHBORHOOD

A little closer
to home.

In her neighborhood, the kids aren’t the only ones who get to enjoy the holiday. While the kids get their sweets, the adults can enjoy tacos and hot dogs, Youngblood said.

“They have various activities and the church folks in my neighborhood are the ones giving out food sometimes,” Youngblood said.

She likes being able to enjoy the holiday with family near her own home rather than having to go elsewhere.

WHICHEVER WAY YOU GO

Different doors.
Same sweet ending.

Both methods work differently for people. Either way, the night still ends with a bag of candy.
